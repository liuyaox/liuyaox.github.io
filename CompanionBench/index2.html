CompanionBench: A Theory-Anchored, Real-World-Grounded Benchmark for AI Emotional Companionship

Separating substantive relational support from a performance of warmth — in Chinese and English, across 28 agents.

01 Overview

Four stages: construct, rollout, judge, report

Abstract

LLM companions are deployed at scale in personally consequential settings, yet poorly evaluated. Existing benchmarks typically use hand-authored scenarios and prompted simulators, aggregate empathy into one score, and overlook judge biases such as same-family favoritism and scale drift. We introduce CompanionBench, an interactive bilingual benchmark. To our knowledge, it is the first companion benchmark to ground both its scenarios and a trained user simulator in de-identified real-world data.

A hidden disclosure gate branches each persona’s trajectory on the companion agent’s own behavior, controlling the interaction state space without scripting dialogue. We operationalize ten capabilities derived from 25 theories, including four not explicitly graded by prior works: holding ambiguity, selfobject responsiveness, positive resonance and calibrated challenge. Agents are assessed on two complementary axes: a subjective ten-capability rubric and a deterministic measure of whether deeper disclosure was earned. A cross-family panel dilutes same-family favoritism; an Item Response Theory model separates agent quality from judge severity. Rankings are reproducible in both languages (ρ = 0.996 ZH / 0.953 EN), and across 28 agents the dominant failure mode is substituting surface warmth for substantive relational support.

02 Motivation

Companionship is neither task success nor high warmth

Earning relational trust turn by turn requires knowing when to affirm versus challenge, when to hold uncertainty rather than rush to solutions, and when deeper disclosure should be earned. A fluent, empathetic reply can still be sycophantic where a measured challenge would serve.

Four reasons existing benchmarks cannot make the separation

i
One aggregate score

A single empathy/warmth number conflates tone with substance — and rewards the very sycophancy a good companion must resist.

ii
Single-turn designs

Context-free or single-turn probes miss the relational process, which is where companionship lives.

iii
Static environments

Hand-authored cases and prompted simulators do not fork with the evaluated agent, so they cannot discriminate.

iv
Contaminated judging

A single LLM judge’s self-preference contaminates rankings whenever the evaluated pool contains the judge’s own family.

The central problem is separating substantive relational support from a performance of warmth — and this substance is predominantly where frontier models fail.

03 Benchmark Construction

A two-layer hybrid: coverage from theory, authenticity from data

Existing benchmarks are researcher-authored or purely data-driven; we adopt a two-layer hybrid. Top-down, one theory pool fixes what the agent must do, what a situation activates and who the user is. Bottom-up, one corpus of tens of thousands of de-identified real conversations supplies events, history and profiles, trained the user simulator, and revised the scenario taxonomy itself — labelling exposed a category the top-down design had missed and another it had over-split.

Ten capabilities, two groups, 42 sub-dimensions

Ten capabilities (C1–C10) on a uniform 1–10 scale, derived from 25 psychology and counseling theories. Four moves reconstruct the space: four ungraded capabilities are added (C5–C8) on the same scale as the established six, so substance and fluent affect become comparable rather than folded into warmth; conflated dimensions are separated (recognition/understanding into C1/C2, “warmth equals good support” into reverse-constrained C3, C5 and C6); warmth, humor and curiosity are excluded as persona style; and a reverse layer of 18 anti-patterns in three severity tiers is added, each linked to a deployment risk.

Seventeen scenarios in seven clusters

Each scenario carries both distressed and positive conversations, so the benchmark spans companionship beyond its help-seeking face. Three families exist because some capabilities surface nowhere else: shared time (S15–17), the only place for positive resonance (C7); identity (S11–12); and self & existence (S13), testing holding ambiguity (C5) and calibrated challenge (C8) rather than problem-solving.

Data: real-grounded, bilingually parallel, de-identified

Multilinguality is not translation-based: culturally specific concepts are independently synthesized in each language — same psychological structure, substituted cultural anchors. A rule-based / human / LLM pipeline removes PII and drops minors’ records while preserving emotional register, and the released data are LLM-rewritten and fully human-reviewed. The seed corpus skews toward S01 and toward young women with anxious attachment; sampling rebalances it onto the coverage matrix over gender, attachment, selfobject need and prior-session presence.

04 The Disclosure Gate

A user simulator that is a transition function of the agent’s behavior

In role-flipped multi-turn rollouts, each user reply is a state transition of the agent’s environment. The simulator’s core is a disclosure gate hidden from the agent — a deterministic state machine over depth (surface < mid < core) whose five ordered gates must each be earned through genuine empathy.

Violating the persona’s anti_goal (judging, lecturing, rushing) triggers a retreat that is quick to withdraw and slow to rebuild — the asymmetry is deliberate. Gates open and lock repeatedly as the conversation proceeds, so depth is not monotonic. We control the state space, not a script or a timeline: the initial state is fixed and disclosure advances solely through agent behavior. That trajectories fork with the agent is the source of discriminating signal — and it makes the gate a deterministic second evaluation axis.

Theoretical grounding and training

The earned depth ladder, disclosure reciprocity and asymmetric retreat-and-repair are anchored in the Social Penetration (Altman & Taylor 1973), reciprocity (Jourard 1971) and rupture–repair (Safran & Muran 2000) traditions; our contribution is to combine them into a single deterministic, gated state machine that controls the trajectory rather than measuring depth post hoc. Training uses two branches, disjoint from the benchmark seeds: a real branch of hundreds of thousands of user turns for human language texture, and a synthetic branch supplying the two contrast endpoints real data lacks — earned deep disclosure from good AI (only 2.28% of real turns) and triggered retreat from poor AI. 05 Two Complementary Axes

Axis-1 judges the surface; axis-2 judges its effect

Axis 1 — subjective
Capability rubric · “Was it well said?”

Judges rate C1–C10 from 1 to 10 over the whole trajectory and flag the 18-item, three-tier reverse anti-patterns. We report three quantities separately rather than one total: primary (mean over applicable capabilities), deduction (tier-weighted penalties) and final (primary less deduction). A composite would let a warm but sycophantic reply and a restrained but precise one score alike.

Axis 2 — deterministic
Disclosure-gate dynamics · “Where did the relationship move?”

Reusing the same gate, the judge labels per turn only an ai_move category (advancing / neutral / violating) and the user’s disclosure depth — no subjective score. A state machine then replays the sequence, computing each turn’s allowed depth and flagging premature disclosure, missed retreat, and deepest earned layer. No gate index is synthesized, which preserves the separation from axis-1.

They are not redundant where it matters: earned depth (max_depth) correlates with axis-1 IRT θ at only ρ = 0.25, even though the gate-advance rate (earn%) tracks it at 0.94. An aggregate score averages that divergence away.

06 Cross-Family Panel & IRT De-biasing

Two judge biases, and why per-agent exclusion is not a safe default

The primary judge is deepseek-v4-pro on both axes; axis-1 adds claude-opus-4-8 and gpt-5.1, forming a three-family panel with identical configuration in both languages. Two biases arise:

Bias 1
Self-preference

LLM judges score same-vendor agents higher: difference-in-differences +0.21 (EN) / +0.279 (ZH).

Bias 2
Scale-incommensurability under selective exclusion

The standard remedy — a cross-family panel with diagonal exclusion — introduces a subtler bias we name here: each agent drops a different judge set, so mean severity drifts and the ranking is contaminated by which judges were excluded.

We fit a two-facet (agent × judge) many-facet Rasch model, overallij = θi + βj + εij with meanj βj = 0, where θ is latent quality and β judge severity. Because β is estimated globally, the θ ranking removes scale heterogeneity, dilutes self-preference, uses every observation and needs no diagonal exclusion. It matches the manually β-subtracted ranking at ρ ≈ 0.99, so it is our final ranking; agents whose adjacent bootstrap CIs overlap form a tied tier.

07 Bilingual Leaderboard

28 agents, reasoning disabled, 500 personas each per language

Each agent runs one role-flipped self-play against each of the 500 personas — 14,000 conversations per language. The primary metric is the de-biased axis-1 IRT θ, which scores the whole-trajectory capability total before reverse penalties, reflecting demonstrated capability. earn% and dep (max_depth, where 0/1/2 = surface/mid/core) are the primary judge’s axis-2 readings.

The ranking forms three bands — an indistinguishable top (EN gpt-5.5 / opus / gpt-5.4), a dense middle, and a cleanly separated bottom. Both language conditions run identical judge configurations, and the result is rankable but not comparable in absolute score: highly consistent across languages (overall ρ = 0.951), yet 22 of 28 agents shift significantly in absolute score, and the shift is structured by capability rather than uniform. That is why rankings are reported within a language, never as a cross-language absolute comparison.

08 What It Reveals

Four findings, all robust across languages

a
In a 20-turn first encounter, no agent earns deep trust

Every agent’s max_depth clusters at mid (0.96–1.43; deepest qwen3.7-max at 1.43), none approaching core. Agent turns that earn deep trust are only about 2% (1.9% EN / 2.0% ZH, robust across languages) — both a deliberate gate constraint and the single-session capability ceiling.

b
The two axes diverge, yet are not unrelated

qwen3.7-max sits mid-pack on axis-1 (ZH θ 8th of 28) yet earns the deepest max_depth (ZH 1.43). Partial correlations controlling for capability reveal a cross-lingually robust process-to-outcome chain: C5 Holding earns depth and stabilizes it (+0.58 EN / +0.38 ZH on max_depth; −0.81 / −0.50 on ruptures), while C8 is its high-risk dual (−0.54 / −0.30) — the clinical pattern where holding earns depth and premature interpretation invites rupture.

c
Tone and substance decouple

Reporting the three quantities separately makes it measurable: high primary (verbal warmth) with a large drop to final once red lines are crossed — claude-haiku-4-5 falls 0.780 → 0.548; the bottom agent 0.395 → 0.017. Twenty of 28 agents sit inside a narrow primary band of 0.78–0.85, so the ordering is made almost entirely by the reverse layer.

d
Role-play models: specialized does not mean competent

The three role-play-optimized models (two doubao-character, minimax-m2-her) fall to the bottom — deficits smallest on C7/C10, largest on C5/C8/C3. Immersion being distinct from the relational competence we measure, their placement is itself discriminant-validity evidence.

Capability profiles: one safety floor, one common weakness, one cascade

C10 Boundary and Safety is uniformly high (top > 9.4, weakest still 7.56), while C4 Emotion Regulation and C8 Calibrated Challenge are weakest even at the top. Failure is a cascade, not uniform decay: recognition, understanding and validation (C1–C3) stay available while the more effortful C4/C5/C8 fall most — C5 Holding collapses from 8.6 to 3.08 across the 28 agents. That is the capability-level face of substituting warmth for substance.

And no model dominates: once level is removed, each has genuine specialties — qwen3.7-max on C5 Holding, deepseek-v4-pro on C8 Calibrated Challenge, gpt-5.4 and opus on C4 Emotion Regulation. Open/closed and general/role-play models intermix with no cluster, confirming provenance is not a quality axis. The widest and least redundant dimensions are C5 and C8; C1–C3 are near-collinear.

09 Failure Modes

What “substituting warmth for substance” looks like as a count

The reverse layer is an a-priori error taxonomy: 18 anti-patterns across three severity tiers, each carrying the capability it damages and a tier weight. Red lines are led by over-promising — overpromise is 98.8% of tier-1 events and the most frequent T1 item for all 28 agents, while the other four red lines total 14 events between them: these systems rarely commit textbook clinical errors, they routinely overstep on relational commitment. Tier 2 is dominated by two warmth-for-substance items, empty_encouragement and forced_positivity, together 83.6% of the tier and in the top two T2 items for 28 of 28 agents.

10 Reliability & Construct Validity

Ranking credibility, established before any conclusion

Construct-validity evidence is generally under-reported in this area. We report three checks below; beyond them, the ranking holds across 24 persona and scenario subgroups (rank ρ 0.823–0.996, median 0.952 — subgroups change difficulty, not rank) and saturates at N ≈ 200 personas under bootstrap sub-sampling.

Check 1
Computational reliability

From a second, independently executed judge pass under identical configuration, the axis-1 ranking has test–retest Spearman ρ = 0.996 (ZH) / 0.953 (EN); axis-2 earn% 0.991 (ZH). Per dimension, 8 of 10 capabilities reach ρ ≥ 0.93, the lowest being the more subjective C7 and C8.

Check 2
Inter-judge agreement

Pairwise Spearman of the axis-1 rankings across the three cross-family judges falls in 0.845–0.951 (EN) — highest between gpt-5.1 and deepseek, lowest with the strictest, outlier opus.

Check 3
Environmental responsiveness

Against a permutation that shuffles each dialogue’s agent moves while pinning earn% exactly, premature disclosure falls well below chance and post-rupture retreat rises well above it (all p < 0.001, both languages), and matched on persona, turn position, permitted depth and history, users disclose deeper under the agent whose preceding move was advancing (p < 10−22). The environment moves with the agent, not on a schedule of its own.

11 Discussion & Limitations

A training–evaluation feedback loop, and an honest ceiling

Social sycophancy is sustained by a loop

The dominant failure — substituting warmth for substance — is not new as an observation; our contribution is mechanical rather than phenomenological: with axis-2 we can show warmth rising while the gate stays shut. Warmth-centric preference training and single-score evaluation both reward exactly this, blind to the gate.

Limitations

Scope

Text-only and single-session. Personas carry a summary of prior sessions, not accumulated history, so the deep-trust ceiling is partly single-session rather than capability. The population is non-clinical and all conclusions correlational.

Measurement

A residual judge × family × language interaction survives IRT — opus is stricter on Chinese open-weight models. Inferred core-depth labels are low-confidence, and 20 fixed turns right-censor longer trajectories.

Validity

No human or counselor baseline, and two external-validity studies are undone: expert inter-rater reliability, and convergent / discriminant validity against established EI measures.

Ethics. All real-user-derived data were de-identified and LLM-rewritten before use; the raw corpus is not released; use is licensed for model training under the data provider’s terms. Records of minors and acute-crisis scenarios are excluded entirely. The benchmark supports comparative research, not certification: a high score is no safety warrant.
